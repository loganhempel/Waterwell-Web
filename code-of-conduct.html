Code of Conduct & complaints

Last updated 3 August 2026

Naturopathy is not a registered profession in Australia. Practitioners in unregistered health professions are instead bound by the National Code of Conduct for Health Care Workers, which sets minimum standards of practice and conduct — and gives you a clear route to complain if those standards are not met.

What the Code requires

Under the National Code, a health care worker must, among other things:

  • provide services in a safe and ethical manner, within their competence and training
  • not make claims about the efficacy of treatment or services, in advertising or otherwise, that cannot be substantiated
  • not attempt to dissuade a client from seeking or continuing medical treatment
  • not mislead a client about the nature of the service or the practitioner's qualifications, training or professional affiliations
  • keep appropriate records and maintain client confidentiality
  • hold appropriate professional indemnity insurance
  • report concerns about another practitioner's conduct
  • make this Code and the complaints process available to clients

This is a summary. The full Code is published by the health complaints body in each state and territory.

How this practice works

  • Naturopathic care here is complementary. It works alongside the care you receive from your doctor and does not replace it.
  • You will never be advised to stop a prescribed medication. If that is something you want to explore, it is a conversation to have with the doctor who prescribed it.
  • Where something needs medical assessment or falls outside what naturopathy can help with, you will be told so and referred on.
  • Consultations are delivered by secure video.

Making a complaint

If you have a concern about your care, please raise it with the clinic first at info@waterwellclinic.co.nz.

You also have the right to complain directly to the health complaints body in your state or territory, and you do not have to contact the clinic first.
